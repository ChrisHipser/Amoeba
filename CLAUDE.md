# Amoeba Drift

A top-down browser game: you start as an amoeba, eat food to level up, and evolve through ten forms. Everything (HTML, CSS, JS, synthesized audio) lives in **one self-contained HTML file**. No build step, no dependencies, no assets.

## Game structure (inside the script)
- **Progression**: `LF[]` is cumulative food per level, up to level ~110 (12 food reaches level 2; amoeba levels 2–10 cost 20% more than v13; levels 5–10 and 15–20 each cost 10% more than the one before (`ramp`); levels 41–70 cost 1/1.3 as much). `gate()` = next evolution level (10, 20 … 90). Levels stop at gate+4 until you press F; surplus food isn't banked. T levels up once (`levelOnce`). `P.size = sqrt(4*level+.5)`; radius `rad() = 12.8 * size * FS[evos]` (the brain also swells 3.5% per level since it evolved, via `P.evoLevel`). Per-level speed steps are 40% smaller than v18 (the `mmps` formula in `frame`).
- **Forms** (`P.evos` 0–`LAST`=9): amoeba, sponge, colony, zooplankton, **urchin, fish** (use `URCH`=4, `FISH`=5), Boltzmann brain, dolphin, `ORCA`=8, `HUMAN`=9 (v19: a cartoon puppet man, `human(R,t,mv,ph,air)`, split-pin joints, crawl stroke, flails in the air). Class names in `CLASS[]`; use the constants and `LAST`, never literals. The dolphin and orca share `dolphin(R,t,mv,w,orca)`; their body wave also runs while wiggling (`waveAmt`). Fish, dolphin, orca and human flip vertically when facing left so they stay upright.
- **Motion**: `motionK()` slows **only the player** by up to 15% from level 10; after each evolution the slowdown resets and eases back in over six levels, so a new form feels quicker. World creatures run at full `dt`.
- **Zoom**: `zoomFor()` eases out per level and jumps ×0.7 per evolution, clamped at `ZMIN` (the orca and human are both at the clamp). Most world speeds are divided by `zoom` so screen speed stays sensible.
- **World**: food lives in 640-unit chunks generated from a seeded hash (`makeChunk`). Density × `foodDens()`: 0.5625 below level 10, 0.75 below 30, 1.6 above 50. Only chunks within ~1.2 view radii are simulated each frame; enemies search and knives hit only nearby chunks. Striders, rods, prey fish and enemies are kept in separate lists and recycled only from off-screen (`ringPos`).
- **Rods and prey fish** share `swimList(list,dt,fleeRadius)`: a heading with capped turn rate/acceleration and rotational twitches; travel direction follows the heading through side grip (`va`), and the tail's beat is proportional to speed. Parameters in `RODP`. Rods are worth 50, shrink ×0.84 per evolution against you, and are gone after the urchin. From the dolphin on, `PREY` holds 10 fish worth 12–90% of `cap()`.
- **Striders**: ×0.75 smaller with every evolution (`striderR`); gone from the dolphin on.
- **Food**: `type` is 'n', 'gold', 'micro' (1-food dots, none after the urchin), 'strider', 'rod', 'prey'. `spawnVal` has no ceiling; from the fish on (`lateFood()`) about 30% of food is out of reach, and out-of-reach food is drawn 1–2× your size whatever its value (`foodDrawR`, `giantK`), fainter and blurrier the bigger it is. Earlier, out-of-reach food is drawn at least your size. Values ≤50 are plain blobs (0–15; shape 10 = bacterium) or tiny pond creatures from `MICRO`; above 50 sea creatures (20–29); deep-sea 34–36 up to 320. Shape 40 is a mitochondrion: its value is capped at `cap()` so it's always edible, its size at `min(ZOOR, rad()*.55)`; blue ones have a tight glow and raise `P.knifeN`.
- **Organelles**: `P.ords` float freely with mutual repulsion; a wiggle shakes them 9× harder. The amoeba's wiggle is a jello wobble (`memR`); other forms up to the brain rotate.
- **Hairs**: rooted just inside the outline through `bodyWarp()` (the same boost squash and wiggle rotation `drawPlayer` applies), and the zooplankton's first segment (`h.s0`, a tenth of a normal segment) is hard-pinned, so they never separate from the membrane and the stiff base is tiny.
- **Evolving** keeps food on screen; the rest of each chunk is dropped, and kept chunks (`stale`) regenerate once out of view.
- **Fractals**: `PROTOS[level]` holds the ten fractals at increasing depth; the layer zooms out by `FRACZ`=0.9 per evolution.
- **Background**: four large painted galaxies (`buildGalaxies`, 512 px, drawn 240–1680 px on screen).
- **Brain** (`brain`): drawn after the user's photo of a real brain from above: two pinkish-beige hemispheres with dense winding folds (`brainSulci`, random-walk Path2Ds built once in unit size) stroked as dark grooves with a red core and a soft crest highlight, and a deep central fissure.
- **Scale**: 1 world unit = 1 mm (`MPU`=1000 per metre). Body lengths: amoeba 5–15 cm, sponge ~30 cm, colony ~35–40 cm, zooplankton ~50 cm, urchin/fish/brain ~1.1–1.7 m, dolphin ~3.8 m, orca ~8 m, human ~6 m. The speed readout uses the same scale: it shows your real top speed (`BASE*mult/zoom*motionK`, in mm/s), about 9 cm/s at the start and ~11 m/s for a full-speed orca.
- **Depth**: shown at the middle left (`#dp`). The water darkens with depth (`dk`) and the caustics fade (`CAUSK`) in `frame`.
- **Sky**: `dayLight()` drives a 3-minute day/night cycle (`DAY`); `drawSky` paints the sky gradient, sun, moon, stars, puffy clouds, the 250 mm lighter band and the sparkle reflection (`SPK` sprite).
- **Surface**: `SURF` (const) is the world y of the water surface, 200 m above the start, always present. `surfH(x)` gives small waves plus rings from `SURFW` disturbances. Above it: you can turn but not steer (`airStep`, gravity `SURFG`), no boost, and game time runs at 0.6×; `checkSurface` handles takeoff (whoosh, `recSlow`: the music brakes to half speed and keeps playing) and landing (`sfx.dive` if you enter within 20° of your heading, else `sfx.flop`; `recRestore`, bubbles). `drawSky` paints the sunset and builds `SPTS` (the surface line); `clipWater()` keeps water things below it. Food, prey and enemies are held below the surface. HUD shows depth/altitude (1 m = 1000 world units).
- **Fish**: tail beats side to side; barbels (`updateWhiskers`) grow over the fish stage.
- **Enemies**: named clones in `ENEMIES` (up to 3), aware about half the time; gain 1× per bite (v19, twice v18). Deadly at ≥2× your food; edible at ≤60%.
- **Knives**: Q or right-click (hold right-click to keep firing, `mouse.rdown`), toward the mouse; a tactical combat knife (`drawKnife`). They only hit enemies (food is untouched): the enemy bleeds ('blood' fx), shrinks 8% and flees for 1.3 s; sound `sfx.bshh`, launch `sfx.throwK`. There is no ink in the game any more.
- **Performance**: adaptive render scale (`RS`, `perfTick`, `applyScale`) drops the canvas resolution to as low as 0.55 when frames take >12 ms and raises it under 6 ms; the haze is painted at 1/8 size (`HZ`); the fractal layer is rebuilt every other frame and slid in between (`SQ`); caustics are blurred once at 1/10 size and drawn in one blit (`cB`); big enemies are tinted on a scratch canvas (`drawTinted`) instead of with `ctx.filter`; off-screen chunks are skipped whole when collecting food to draw.
- **Colony**: 29 fat, squishy cells (stretch along their motion); the outer ring of 10 has shimmering rows of flagella. **Sponge** is white. **Brain** wings are seagull wings (`wing` returns the tip; contrails in `CTR`, `addContrail`, `drawContrails`). **Orca**: notched fin, thick tail stock, toothy mouth and slanted brow. **Human**: red cap, goggles, mitten hands, small feet, cartoon crack.
- **Food sizes (fish on)**: out-of-reach food is drawn up to 2× your size, cap halved for the dolphin and quartered for the orca/human (`foodDrawR`).
- **Effects**: progress ring at `rad()+188 px`. Boost sheds `BW` ripples; boost bubbles only from the urchin on. Spray drops ('drop' fx) at the surface.
- **HUD**: name, CLASS, speed (real top speed, on the depth meter's scale), depth, SCORE, LEVEL. `drawHudIcon` redraws the current form by swapping the global `ctx`.
- **Audio**: `OUT` (VOL 0.6) → `REC` delay → `RLP` lowpass → `RG` gain → speakers; `recSlow` ramps the delay so the whole mix grinds down like a record while you are airborne. `FXD` bypasses it (whoosh, big splash). M mutes `OUT` and `FXD`. `pondAmbience` (all forms): unpitched drips and soft noise washes. `pondLayers`/`pondMusic` (forms 0–5): amoeba deep bloops; sponge + swells; colony deep underwater rumble + long low pads on the arpeggio's chord (`longPad`, `arpF`); zooplankton + low rushing-water swells; urchin echoing bells on the arpeggio + an island-rock groove (`calypso`: kick, snare, bass on the arpeggio's root); fish sonar pings, currents, bubble trails + long pads. Brain pads, dolphin/orca whale song, human body percussion (`humanBeat`). `PROGS` has 15 progressions (triads MAJ/MIN/SUS added to `CHORDS`). Eating: piano note (`harpBuf`) over `PROGS`, plus a tiny `eatBloop` (also for micro food). Note limit (`NOTES`): after any note at most 3 more within 0.15 s; after an arpeggio's last note at most 2 more within 0.2 s; suppressed bites don't advance the arpeggio. Faint boost splash (`bsplash`, louder from the fish on).

## Controls
WASD/arrows move · click/hold to swim toward the pointer · Shift sprint (1.5×, 4 s, 4.5 s recharge) · Space boost (2 s) · Q or right-click knife toward mouse (hold right-click to keep firing) · E wiggle + lure · F evolve · T level up once · 4 then 4 skip evolution · P pause · M mute · Y start over. Touch: hold to swim toward the finger.

## Known history worth remembering
- **The "drumbeat" bug** was the eating schlorp being queued instead of dropped. Any new per-eat sound must skip, not queue (`schEnd`, `lvlEnd`, `chompEnd`, `bassEnd`, `bloopEnd`, `kpopEnd`).
- An old 55 Hz drone and a 20-second loop both caused hums. Don't reintroduce continuous oscillators; long sounds (pads, swells) must always end. `longPad` keeps pitches between 90 and 200 Hz.
- Hairs: sponge = loose string, zooplankton = slightly elastic string with heavy water drag. Neither should be springy.
- The dolphin's body wave uses an accumulated phase (`P.dph`; the human's stroke uses it too).
- Never put a `//` comment in the middle of a minified line: it silently comments out the rest. This broke v15, v16 and twice during v19's edits (caught by `cmt.py`-style checks). When editing the start of a line, end your inserted comment with `/* */`.
- Overlapping subpaths in one fill must wind the same way, or the overlap becomes a hole.
- The files use CRLF line endings.
- Caustics cost ~11 ms per frame in the hidden test pane (software canvas) with or without clipping; that's not a v19 regression.

## Testing
Node isn't installed on this PC; Python is. Test in the in-app browser through a local server (a small Python `http.server` that also accepts `POST /save/<name>.png` and answers `/favicon.ico` with 204). The pane is hidden, so: inject code with a `<script>` element (the JS tool can't see the page's top-level `let`/`const`), set `W=1400;H=900` and resize the canvas yourself, then step `frame()` by hand and save `cv.toDataURL()` crops to look at. A Python bracket-balance check of the script catches unclosed blocks fast.
**Never make sound while testing:** override `ensureAudio=function(){}` first, and close the game tabs afterwards. To exercise audio code silently, replace `window.AudioContext` with a function returning an `OfflineAudioContext` before calling `ensureAudio()`, then `startRendering()` and inspect the buffer; it never reaches the speakers.
`tools/` (in `..\Amoeba Project\tools`, needs Node + Playwright) is written for v13 and out of date.
**No screenshots** (user's rule since v19): don't capture or view images to preview changes. Test with code checks only (bracket balance, mid-line `//` scan, load the page and step `frame()` for errors, offline audio render), and let the user judge visuals in the game.
**Edit in place:** change the game file with targeted edits; never rewrite the whole file.
